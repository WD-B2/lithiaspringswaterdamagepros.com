Carpet can often be saved, and sometimes it cannot

Carpet water damage restoration in Lithia Springs, GA is a sorting job. Clean water from a supply line or an overflowing tub often leaves carpet that can be extracted and dried in place. Water that picked up dirt, drain contents or sewage almost never does. A failed tank is a frequent culprit, and water heater leak cleanup covers that source.

Time works against you either way. Pad soaks up water like a sponge and holds it against the subfloor, so the first hours decide whether the carpet is a candidate for saving. If water is spreading right now, call (943) 469-0138 and keep reading while you wait.

The deciding question: what kind of water got into the carpet?

Restoration standards sort water into three categories, and the category sets what is done with the carpet.

Water sourceCategoryCarpet decision
Broken supply line, tub overflow, sink overflow1 (clean at the source)Extract and dry in place when caught quickly
Washer or dishwasher drain, sump overflow, rain through a wall2 (significant contamination)Pad removed, carpet cleaned and dried or replaced
Sewage, river or creek flood water3 (grossly contaminated)Carpet and pad removed and discarded
Clean water left standing past a day or twoMoves toward 2Pad usually discarded, carpet judged on readings

Category can worsen with time. Clean water becomes questionable as bacteria grow in warm, wet material.

Signs that carpet water damage is deeper than it looks

Squishing underfoot

If water rises around your shoe, the pad is saturated and the subfloor is wet beneath it.

Rippling or wrinkling

Wrinkles mean the backing has stretched. Re-stretching works only after the carpet and pad are dry.

Stains wicking upward

Brown marks climbing along walls or furniture legs show moisture traveling upward through the fibers.

A sour, stale odor

Smell often shows up before any stain. It signals that wet pad or subfloor is staying wet.

What happens to wet carpet, step by step

  1. Extract the bulk water

    A truck mounted or portable extractor with a carpet wand pulls water from fibers and pad. Weighted tools press out water that suction alone misses.

  2. Lift and inspect the pad

    A section of carpet is peeled back from the tack strip so the pad and the subfloor can be read with a moisture meter.

  3. Dry the structure

    Air movers push air under the lifted carpet while LGR dehumidifiers remove the moisture from the room. Drying equipment is placed by the readings.

  4. Clean, deodorize and re-lay

    Once readings match a dry reference, carpet is cleaned, pad replaced if needed and the carpet is stretched back onto the tack strip.

The Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority describes itself as the county's sole water provider, handling water, wastewater and stormwater. If the water in your carpet came from a main or a service line before your meter, or from a backed-up sewer, the utility is part of the picture.

Call the Authority about the line and call our dispatch line about the carpet. Do not wait for one to finish before starting the other, because wet pad ages while you sit on hold.

Save the carpet or replace it: how the trade-off works

Saving carpet is cheaper when the water was clean and the pad can be dried or swapped quickly. Replacement costs more in materials but avoids the odor and delamination risks that come with old, soaked backing. Wool and natural fiber carpets also shrink or discolor in ways nylon does not.

Pad is a smaller part of the bill than carpet, so many crews replace the pad even when the carpet stays. Carpet over a damaged floor is a different job, and our pages for structural drying and dehumidification and hardwood floor water damage repair cover the cases where the carpet is only part of the story.

What drives the cost on a carpet job

  • Water category

    Category 3 means removal and disposal, which is a larger scope than drying.

  • Size of the wet area

    A single room is a small loss, but water often travels into hallways and closets.

  • Pad and carpet type

    Standard nylon cut pile is easier to dry than looped berber or wool.

  • Subfloor and tack strip

    Plywood subfloor that has soaked can need extra drying time or replacement.

Common Questions

How soon does carpet need to be dealt with?

As soon as possible. Clean water that stays in carpet and pad past a day or so begins to grow bacteria and may be treated as contaminated. Faster extraction means more options.

Can I dry carpet with a fan and open windows?

Surface fibers dry that way, but the pad and subfloor usually do not. In humid weather, open windows can add moisture. A moisture meter tells you if the underside is truly dry.

Will the carpet shrink or smell after drying?

Properly dried and cleaned carpet should not hold a lingering odor. Stretching may be needed to remove ripples. Smell that returns is a sign that the pad or subfloor stayed wet.

Is it safe to walk on wet carpet?

Avoid it if wet areas are near outlets, power strips or lamps, and keep children and pets out of contaminated water. Turn power off only if you can reach the panel while standing on something dry.

What if I can not tell whether the water was clean?

Treat it as unknown. Ask the crew to explain how they sorted the water category, and keep samples of the paperwork. When in doubt, pad comes out.

Do you take the old carpet away?

Yes, when carpet or pad has to go, the crew rolls it, bags it and hauls it out. Ask beforehand whether disposal is part of the written scope so there are no surprises.
