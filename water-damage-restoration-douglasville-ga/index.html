Douglasville by road

Find yourself by road, then by room

Veterans Memorial Highway carries US 78 and State Route 8 through Douglasville, and Chapel Hill Road, Fairburn Road, Campbellton Street and Hospital Drive cross the city. Interstate 20 exits 34, 36 and 37 serve it. When you phone in, name the road and the nearest exit, since those two details tell a dispatcher more than a street number alone.

After the address comes the question of where the water sits. A kitchen with a cracked supply line is a different job from a retail unit with a roof leak. If water covers the floor, pulling it out with pumps and extraction units has to happen before any drying begins.

Four kinds of building along Douglasville's corridors

Douglasville is a county seat with a historic core, a mill neighborhood, a large regional mall and a high share of rented homes. Dispatch is open around the clock for all of them, and each building type calls for a slightly different plan.

Downtown brick

The Downtown Douglasville Historic District is on the National Register and holds one and two story brick buildings in Victorian commercial, Romanesque, Italianate and Queen Anne styles. Brick and old plaster hold water, so moisture readings matter more and drying runs longer.

Mill Village streets

Mill Village Park on Grady Street marks the old mill neighborhood. Older houses there often sit over crawl spaces, so a leak beneath the floor can wet subfloor and insulation before the room above shows a stain.

Chapel Hill Road and Arbor Place

Arbor Place, a regional mall of about 4 million square feet at I-20 and Chapel Hill Road, anchors a commercial strip. In buildings that large, a sprinkler or roof drain failure spreads across shared floors into neighboring units quickly.

Apartments and rentals

About 48 percent of Douglasville homes are owner-occupied according to American Community Survey data, so many residents rent. A leak in one apartment reaches the unit below through the ceiling, which makes a call to the property manager as urgent as a call to us.

Courthouse square and the 1901 house on Campbellton Street

The Douglas County Museum of History and Art occupies the Old Courthouse at 12431 Veterans Memorial Highway. The Cultural Arts Center fills the 1901 Roberts-Mozley House at 8652 Campbellton Street. Both are the sort of older building where plaster, old brick and original floors change how water must be dried.

The city began as Skint Chestnut, and regular Georgia Pacific Railway service to Atlanta started in 1882. That age is a reason to tell the dispatcher when your building went up and what the walls are made of, so the crew can plan for plaster or brick and not for modern drywall.

When water is spreading in Douglasville

If water is crossing a floor, running down a stairwell or dripping through a ceiling in Douglasville, treat it as an emergency and dial (943) 469-0138. Crews are sent out at night and on weekends too, and same-day response is offered when available.

On a commercial or multi-unit floor, tell the dispatcher how many units are wet and whether a sprinkler is flowing. If a fire sprinkler is discharging, call 911 first. Water loss in a business or apartment building needs a record of every room that took water.

If water is still spreading

Before the crew is on the way, these six actions limit the loss.

Find the source

Look for the broken fixture, hose or pipe and close its own shutoff. If water is coming through a ceiling, the source is above you, so alert whoever lives or works there.

Main valve

Close the main water valve at the house or building. In an apartment or commercial building, find the manager, because the riser valve may not be inside your unit.

Electric caution

Leave breakers alone if you must stand in water to reach them. A ceiling holding water over a light fixture is unsafe, so keep out of that room.

Move what you can

Clear floors, lift boxes onto shelves and pull rugs off wet areas. Photograph the damage before you move items, since insurers often ask for documentation.

Protect people and pets

Drain overflow and anything that touched outdoor ground is unsafe water. Send children and pets to a dry room.

Water, permits and who to ask in Douglasville

The Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority, created in 1985 and headquartered at 8763 Hospital Drive, is described as the county's sole water provider. If you cannot find a working shutoff or suspect a leak on the street side of the meter, that authority is the one to call about the line.

For repairs that need permits, the City of Douglasville Community Development Building Division handles building, electrical, plumbing and HVAC permits. Ask it before you replace wiring, move plumbing or swap HVAC equipment after a loss.

Common Questions

Is emergency water removal available in Douglasville?

Yes. Dispatch at (943) 469-0138 never closes, so a night or weekend call is handled like a weekday one, and same-day response is offered when available. Say what the building is, what road it is on and where the water started, and the dispatcher can line up pumps, extraction units and drying equipment before anyone drives out.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Douglasville?

Yes, both street ZIPs. ZIP 30135 holds the larger share of residents. ZIP 30134 covers downtown and government addresses such as the Old Courthouse museum on Veterans Memorial Highway and the Water and Sewer Authority office on Hospital Drive. Post office box ZIPs are not delivery points, so give the crew your street address.

A pipe broke in my apartment. Do I call you or the property manager?

Both. The property manager can close the building riser and notify the unit below, and the 24/7 line sends extraction and drying help. Name your unit and floor, and take photos of every room that got wet before things are moved.

Will old plaster or brick downtown take longer to dry?

Usually yes. Brick and lime plaster hold moisture longer than modern drywall, and a wall cavity can stay wet after the surface looks dry. Readings are logged every visit until materials reach dry standard. If a wall has to be opened, wall and drywall repair comes after the cavity tests dry.

Who do I call about a water main or meter problem in Douglasville?

The Douglasville-Douglas County Water and Sewer Authority handles the water system, so a leak on the street side of the meter is its concern. A leak past the meter, inside your walls or under your slab, belongs to you and a restoration crew. If you cannot tell which side the leak is on, call the authority first.

Is flood damage from a creek or storm covered by my homeowners policy?

Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage. Sudden and accidental water, such as a failed supply line, is more often covered, and gradual leaks often are not. Check your policy, ask your insurer, and look up your address on the FEMA flood map.
