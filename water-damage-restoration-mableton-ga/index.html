Creek first

Nickajack Creek, 19.85 feet and the homes beside it

Nickajack Creek runs from Marietta through Smyrna to its mouth on the Chattahoochee River in Mableton. According to the National Weather Service, the Mableton gauge crested at 19.85 feet in 2009 against a flood stage of 10 feet, and the creek stayed above flood stage across three days.

A creek that sits high for days saturates the soil around it, so a low room on a nearby lot can take on seepage long after the rain ends. Wind-driven rain through a roof or siding is a different case and is handled as storm water cleanup, while groundwater coming through a floor is a below-grade problem.

Heritage Park, the mill ruins and the river edge

Heritage Park at 60 Fontaine Road has a 1.7 mile trail along Nickajack Creek that passes the Concord Woolen Mills ruins. The Chattahoochee River forms part of the city border, and the Silver Comet Trail crosses town with a trailhead at Concord Road.

If your home backs onto a trail, a creek bank or low ground near the river, say so on the call. Soil near water stays wet longer than ground on a ridge, and that detail tells a crew to bring moisture meters for the lowest rooms first.

Built in 1843 or built last year: why house age matters

The Robert Mable House at 5239 Floyd Road dates to 1843 and sits on the National Register. Beyond it, Mableton has homes from many decades, and no public figure here gives a typical age. Find out when yours went up, because the era decides which pipes, walls and floors the water met.

Under the floor: crawl spaces and below-grade rooms

Where groundwater rises, the lowest space shows it first. Moisture beneath a raised floor can go unseen for weeks, while a flooded basement announces itself at once.

A sump pump that never rests or a musty smell from the vents are signs to call before the next storm. A moisture meter and thermal imaging camera locate wet insulation and subfloor without opening every wall.

If water is still spreading

These six steps buy time, and none of them requires tools.

Cut the supply

Close the valve under the fixture or behind the appliance. If the line feeding it is the problem, move on to the main.

Shut the main

Turn off the main water valve. Cobb Water, per the city's resident guide, handles water issues, so contact it if the meter valve is the only shutoff.

Mind the electricity

Do not touch switches or cords in a wet room. If the panel is dry and reachable, turn off the breakers for that area and no others.

Raise belongings

Put electronics, books and papers on tables or upper shelves. Pull rugs and lift furniture off wet carpet if you can do it safely.

Avoid contaminated water

Water from a creek, a toilet or a floor drain can carry sewage. Keep children and pets out of it and wash hands after any contact.

Place the call

Dial (943) 469-0138. Give the street, the cross street and whether the water came from inside, the roof or the ground.

Creek water and electricity do not mix

Do not wade through water that has come in from a creek or yard, especially if outlets, an electrical panel or a furnace sit below the waterline. If you smell gas or see sparking, leave and call 911 before the restoration line.

Cobb County fire Precinct 2 serves Mableton, and any fire or gas emergency starts with 911, not with a restoration call.

Cobb Water, the city and repair permits

Mableton's resident guide says Cobb Water handles water issues, since the city does not run a water system. If a shutoff will not turn or you suspect a failing main, contact Cobb Water before the line gets worse.

For repairs that need a permit, the City of Mableton Building Department takes applications online through Cloud Permit, with its office at the Riverside EpiCenter, 135 Riverside Parkway, second floor. Ask what your repair needs before walls are closed.

Common Questions

Is emergency water removal available in Mableton?

Yes. Call (943) 469-0138 at any hour, including overnight and on holidays, and the dispatcher sends professionals when the water is still moving. Same-day response is offered when available. Say whether the water came from a pipe, the roof or the ground, and whether any room has standing water.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Mableton?

Mableton's own ZIP is 30126, and it is covered, from the Mable House area on Floyd Road to Heritage Park on Fontaine Road. The city limits drawn in 2022 are wider than one ZIP and some addresses inside them carry another post office name. If that is you, give the street and cross street and coverage can be confirmed by phone.

Did cityhood change who I call for water or permits?

Water stays with Cobb Water, which the city's resident guide names for water issues. Permits now go through the City of Mableton Building Department, and fire service comes from Cobb County Precinct 2. Confirm details with those offices, since the city is new and procedures may change.

Does Nickajack Creek flooding put my house in a flood zone?

A creek crest in 2009 does not by itself place your address in a flood zone. Check the FEMA flood map for your street and ask your insurer about flood coverage, since standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood. Sudden and accidental water from inside the house is more often covered.

Does the age of my house change the drying plan?

Yes. Plaster and plank subfloors hold water longer than modern drywall, and older homes may have more layers to dry. Daily readings decide when equipment leaves, and drying the structure continues until materials read dry.

What should I tell the dispatcher when I call from Mableton?

Give the street, the nearest cross street or corridor, the age of the house and where the water started. Say if anyone is in the water, if power is on in the wet area and if the water looks clean, brown or gray. Those answers decide the equipment.
