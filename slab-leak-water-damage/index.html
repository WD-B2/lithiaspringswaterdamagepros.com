Emergency Slab Leak Water Damage in Lithia Springs, GA

A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs under or inside a concrete foundation, and the water often rises through the floor before anyone sees it. Slab leak water damage in Lithia Springs, GA is a real emergency when it surfaces, and the 24/7 line takes the call. It differs from a burst pipe because the water starts out of sight.

If you see water coming up through the floor, shut the main first and treat it as an emergency

This is an emergency whenever water surfaces, and dispatch runs around the clock at (943) 469-0138. While you wait, close the main water valve at the house or the meter, lift rugs and valuables off the floor, and keep everyone out of the wet room. Switch off power to that area only if the panel is reachable on dry floor.

Closing the main stops the supply line from feeding the leak. It does not drain what is already under the slab, so water can keep surfacing for a while. Take photos of the wet spots and of the water meter before cleanup begins.

A warm spot on the floor, a high water bill or the sound of running water with every tap off?

Those three signs are what a slab leak looks like from inside the house. Each one points to a leak that the eye cannot reach, and each deserves a call to a plumber and a moisture check. Slab leak water damage restoration is the drying and rebuilding of whatever those leaks reach.

Warm or hot patch on the floor

A hot water line under the slab is leaking and heating the concrete above it. The spot is often small, and the floor covering over it may buckle or stain.

Water bill jumps with no change in use

Water is leaving the system somewhere you cannot see. Compare the meter reading with every fixture off, and if the dial moves, there is a leak.

Sound of running or hissing water

Listen in a quiet moment near walls and floors. A steady sound with every fixture closed suggests a pressurized line is releasing.

Damp carpet, lifting flooring or a musty smell

Moisture is coming up through the slab or along the wall base. These show after the leak has run for a while.

What to do while you wait for the crew

Close the main

Shut the home's main water valve, then open one faucet to relieve pressure. If the leak stops showing, the line under the floor is the likely source.

Protect the electrics

Keep away from outlets and cords in wet rooms. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, shoes and low furniture onto dry surfaces. Foil or plastic under furniture legs keeps stains from setting.

Note the meter

Write down the water meter reading with everything off. A reading that keeps moving after the main is closed points to a leak before the meter, so mention it on the call.

How a pipe under concrete ends up leaking

Supply lines under a slab sit in soil or in a bed of sand. Copper can pit and corrode from the outside, especially with certain soils and water chemistry. Lines rub against rough gravel or concrete as they expand and contract with hot water. Joints and fittings under the slab also fail.

High water pressure adds stress to every joint, and a pressure reducing valve at the house helps. A ground that shifts slightly or settles around the foundation can pinch a line. The result is a pinhole or a split that can run for months before water finds a way up through a crack or a joint.

Gas and drain lines can also run under a slab, and a leaking drain adds contamination to the problem. Odd smells, as opposed to just water, need a separate call to the gas utility or a plumber.

How the leak is found, fixed and dried

Restoration crews dry and rebuild. A licensed plumber finds and fixes the line, and the two jobs have to be coordinated.

  1. Confirm the leak

    A plumber isolates the system with a pressure test and uses acoustic listening equipment or a thermal camera to locate the line under the slab.

  2. Choose a repair path

    Options are breaking the slab to reach the pipe, rerouting the line through walls or attic, or lining the pipe. Each has different cost and disruption.

  3. Dry the structure

    Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and floor drying systems pull moisture from carpet, wall bases and the slab surface. Moisture meters track progress, since concrete holds water for a long time.

  4. Rebuild finishes

    Pad, baseboards, drywall and flooring are replaced where drying did not save them. Wet concrete may be left to dry fully before new flooring goes down.

Reroute, repipe or repair in place

Plumbers typically offer three paths. Breaking through the slab to fix the one leak is direct but opens the floor. Rerouting the line overhead avoids the slab and can be less messy, but it adds new pipe through walls or the attic. Repiping the whole house makes sense when the lines are old and failing in more than one place.

Trenchless lining and epoxy coating exist for some pipes, and a plumber can say whether they suit your layout. No option is right for every house. Ask for the options in writing, along with how each affects the drying and the floors.

What shapes the cost of slab leak damage

  • How long it ran

    A leak caught early wets a patch. A leak that ran for months has soaked pad, walls and slab.

  • Area and floor type

    Carpet is quick to lift. Wood and tile need more care.

  • Water category

    Supply water is clean at first. A leaking drain line is a different category and a larger job.

  • Drying days

    Concrete holds water, and drying times run longer than on framing.

  • Plumbing path

    Spot repair, reroute and repipe are plumber costs separate from restoration.

Sudden and accidental plumbing leaks are often covered for the resulting damage, while gradual leaks and the cost to access the pipe often are not. Common Questions

Why is my floor warm and my water bill so high?

A warm spot on a slab floor can mean a hot water line is leaking below it, and a high bill means water is leaving the system. Turn off every fixture and watch the meter. If the dial moves, call a plumber to pressure test the lines, and call the restoration line if any water has surfaced.

Do you handle slab leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Lithia Springs?

Yes. If water is coming up through the floor at night, call then. Dispatch is open around the clock and offers same-day response when available. Close the main valve first, then call (943) 469-0138. Crews also cover Austell and surrounding towns.

Are slab leaks common in Lithia Springs?

The research for this page found no local data on how many homes sit on slabs, so no one can say how common they are here. They happen wherever a home has a slab with pressurized lines under it. Check your foundation type and your meter.

Will insurance cover a slab leak?

Often the water damage from a sudden leak is covered while the cost of reaching and repairing the pipe may not be. Gradual leaks are often excluded. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

Can mold grow from a slab leak?

Mold needs moisture and time, and a wet slab or wall base provides both. Nobody can diagnose it without testing. Mold work follows the S520 standard, and health questions belong to a physician.

How long does a slab take to dry?

Concrete holds water longer than wood framing, so expect it to take longer than a wall. The crew logs readings until the slab and surrounding materials are dry. New flooring should wait for those numbers.
