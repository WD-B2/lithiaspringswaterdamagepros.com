A pipe just burst and water is still running

This is the kind of call that cannot wait until morning, and our line answers 24/7. Water from a supply line does not stop on its own, so shutting the valve is the single most useful thing you can do before anyone arrives.

This page is about burst pipe water damage in Lithia Springs, GA, meaning the pressurized supply side of your plumbing. Drain and sewer failures are a different problem with different risks, and a split caused by ice has its own page on frozen pipe damage.

The first ten minutes after a pipe lets go

Close the main valve

Turn the home's main shutoff clockwise. If it is stuck or outside, the meter valve is the backup.

Drain the line

Open the lowest faucet in the house and flush a toilet so leftover pressure is released instead of leaking.

Power only if safe

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you are standing on dry ground.

Lift and move

Pull valuables, papers and electronics above the water line. Keep people and pets out of any water near outlets.

Why a pressurized line floods a house so fast

Household supply lines usually carry pressure somewhere around 40 to 80 psi. A split that looks small can release many gallons an hour, and the stream is driven sideways into insulation and framing.

Drywall wicks water upward, often a foot or more above the visible wet line. By the time a stain shows on the ceiling below, the wall cavity above has been soaking for a while.

Where supply lines tend to fail

Most bursts trace back to one of a few weak points.

Fittings and connectors

Braided washer hoses, ice maker lines and compression fittings under sinks give way more often than the pipe itself.

Pinhole corrosion

Older metal lines thin from the inside until one spot opens under normal pressure.

Pressure spikes

Water hammer or a high-pressure street feed stresses joints that were fine for years.

Impact damage

A nail, a drill bit or a shifting load during remodeling can nick a line that fails days later.

What the age of Lithia Springs housing means for your pipes

Douglas County's Lithia Springs Small Area Plan says most residential development here dates from about 1940 to 1980, with some infill in the 1990s and 2000s. Homes of that era can still have original galvanized steel or older copper lines.

Ask a licensed plumber what material your supply lines are, and replace braided hoses on washers and fixtures before they fail. The county building permit office can tell you when a repipe needs a permit.

Electrical and ceiling hazards after a burst

Water can run through light fixtures and along wiring. If a ceiling is bulging, stay out from under it. A sagging ceiling can drop its load without warning.

If lights flicker, outlets spark or you smell burning, call 911 or the utility first and the restoration line second. Do not touch a switch with wet hands.

What happens once the water is off

  1. Extract

    Pumps and extraction units remove standing water, the same work described on the water extraction page.

  2. Open

    Small holes or flood cuts in drywall let air reach wet cavities. Baseboards come off so trapped water can escape.

  3. Dry

    Air movers push dry air across surfaces while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room.

  4. Verify

    Meters are read daily until readings return to the dry standard, and equipment stays until they do.

What changes the cost of a burst pipe job

  • How long it ran

    A line shut off in minutes soaks less than one that ran overnight.

  • Materials hit

    Carpet, hardwood and drywall have different dry-or-replace limits.

  • Rooms and floors

    Water that traveled through two levels needs more drying equipment.

  • Drying days

    The number of air movers and dehumidifiers and how long they run is a main driver.

  • Rebuild scope

    Replacing drywall, insulation, paint and trim is separate from mitigation.

Repair now or wait for the full scope

Plumbing repair and water mitigation are different trades. A plumber fixes the line, and restoration crews dry the building. Do the first as soon as possible, but do not wait on it to start the second.

Kitchens take the worst of it when the supply line is under a sink, which is covered on the kitchen water damage page. Walls that stay damp need the steps on the drywall repair page.

Common Questions

Do you handle burst pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Lithia Springs?

Yes. Pipes tend to fail overnight, and the line is staffed for exactly that. Weekend and holiday calls go out the same way, with same-day response offered when available. Call (943) 469-0138 and close the main valve while you wait.

How do I find my main water shutoff?

Look near where the supply enters the house, often by the water heater, in a garage, or at a hose bib. The meter box at the street has a second valve, which the Douglasville-Douglas County Water & Sewer Authority controls, so use your own valve first.

Should I take photos before cleanup?

Yes, if it is safe. Georgia's insurance commissioner suggests photographing or recording damage first and keeping receipts for temporary repairs. Do not make permanent repairs before an inspection if you plan to file a claim.

Will my ceiling need replacing?

Not always. A ceiling that is stained but firm may dry in place, while one that sags or bubbles usually needs to come down so trapped water can drain.

How long does drying take after a burst?

Most structures need about three to five days of equipment, but thick materials and multi-level jobs run longer. Meter readings decide when equipment leaves, not a calendar.
