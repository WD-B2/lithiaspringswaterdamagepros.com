Water in a business: what to do before the crew gets there

A leak in a commercial building is urgent because it touches inventory, equipment, tenants and your ability to open. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and standing water comes out first, see water extraction. While you wait, shut the water main or the zone valve, move inventory and electronics off the floor, and keep staff and customers out of any room with standing water.

Photograph the damage and the source before anything is moved. Write down the time you noticed it. Note which tenants or units are affected, because a building often has more than one responsible party.

Electricity, gas and fire systems in a wet building

Commercial buildings hold three-phase panels, server racks, and kitchen equipment, so water near any of them is dangerous. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing in a dry area. If water is on live equipment, you smell gas, or sprinklers have discharged, call 911 or the utility before you call our line.

Do not enter a room where the ceiling is bulging with water. Wet ceiling tile and drywall can drop without warning.

How commercial water losses differ from a house

Larger areas, flat floors

Open slabs and long corridors spread water across a wide footprint, so extraction units and air movers are needed in bulk.

Operating while we dry

Work is phased so a shop can keep selling, a clinic can keep seeing patients, or a unit can stay occupied.

Shared walls and floors

In multi-unit buildings, water travels down through floors and sideways through shared walls, so neighbors need checking.

Different materials

Commercial carpet tile, suspended ceilings, steel studs and concrete react to water differently than residential finishes.

How we handle a commercial loss from call to dry

Crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Your scope may differ.

  1. Triage and scope

    The dispatcher gathers the building type, water source and affected units. A crew arrives and uses moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to map wetness.

  2. Extract and protect

    Extraction units clear water. Contents are moved or blocked off, and affected tile, ceiling panels and wet insulation are removed.

  3. Dry in phases

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed to keep drying zones separate from working zones. See the structural drying page.

  4. Document and release

    Moisture logs and photos support your insurance paperwork. Rooms are released once readings meet standard.

After hours, weekends and holidays

Pipes do not break on a schedule. Crews dispatch around the clock, and same-day response is offered when available. A night call also means you can sort out downtime before opening hour. Ask the dispatcher what the first visit will cover.

Many businesses call only after the mess has been sitting a day. Do not wait, since porous finishes start to fail within the first day or two. Early calls keep the scope smaller.

What drives commercial restoration cost

Square footage alone does not set the total. Look at these.

  • Floor area and number of units

    More wet area needs more equipment and labor.

  • Water category

    A clean supply line costs less to fix than a drain or flood.

  • Building materials

    Concrete, carpet tile and drywall dry at different speeds.

  • Phasing needs

    Working around open hours or tenants adds planning.

  • Rebuild scope

    Permits and finish work are separate from mitigation. Ask the Douglas County building department which projects need a permit.

Commercial property policies differ, and business interruption coverage is a separate line. Check your policy or ask your insurer. For a number that fits your building and your downtime, call (943) 469-0138.

Common Questions

Do you handle commercial water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Lithia Springs?

Yes. A break after closing is common, so after-hours calls are routine for a commercial loss. Dispatch is open nights, weekends and holidays, and same-day response is offered when available. Call (943) 469-0138 and describe the building and what is wet.

Can you work while my business stays open?

Often yes. Crews phase the work, isolate wet zones, and place equipment so part of the space stays usable. The plan depends on where the water is.

I rent my space. Who calls?

Tell your landlord or property manager first, since the lease usually sets who is responsible. Both can call our line to describe the loss.

What if the water came from a neighbor unit?

Water travels through floors and shared walls. Photograph the damage, notify management, and check your own policy. Crews can map moisture on your side.

How do you document a loss for insurance?

Crews take photos and keep moisture logs for each visit. You still file the claim yourself, and your insurer decides coverage.

How long until we can reopen?

It depends on the area, materials and readings. Phased work often lets part of a building reopen sooner, while rooms with removal or contamination wait for clearance.
