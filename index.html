First 24 to 72 hours

What happens in the first 24 to 72 hours after water gets in

Timing matters because wet drywall, framing and subfloor soak up moisture fast, and mold needs moisture to take hold. These are physics windows, not arrival promises. What follows is the usual order of work on a typical household loss.

  1. Hour 0 to 2: stop and protect

    Close the main water valve, lift valuables off the floor and switch off power to wet areas only if the panel is reached dry. Then call. Dispatch will ask what the water is, where it came from and how far it has spread.

  2. Hours 2 to 12: extract and assess

    Crews pull standing water with pumps and extraction units, then map moisture with meters and a thermal imaging camera. Wet carpet padding is often cut out and discarded because it holds water like a sponge.

  3. Day 1 to 3: dry the structure

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed so moisture leaves the materials and not only the air. Technicians return to read the meters and move or pull equipment as the numbers fall.

  4. Day 3 and after: confirm and repair

    Readings are compared against dry, unaffected material before equipment leaves. Repair comes next, from drywall and paint to flooring, and its scope is explained before it starts.

Not every job follows this clock. A job that is mostly standing water removal front-loads the work, while drying a slab-on-grade floor or a hardwood floor can stretch well past three days.

Steps to take while you wait for the crew

You can lower the damage before anyone arrives. These four actions cost nothing and matter most in the first hour.

Stop the source

Turn off the appliance, close the fixture shutoff or clamp the leaking line if you can do it dry and safely.

Shut the main valve

Know where the main water valve is before you need it. Closing it stops a supply line failure from running all night.

Power off wet areas, if safe

Only cut power from a panel you can reach standing on dry ground. If water is near outlets or the panel itself, call the utility or 911 first.

Lift valuables, keep people out

Move electronics, papers and small furniture up. Keep children and pets away from water that came from a toilet, drain or creek.

What drives the cost of water damage restoration

No one can responsibly quote a number from a headline or a text message. The price follows the facts of the loss, and the same room can cost very different amounts depending on these five things.

  • Water category

    Clean water is simpler to handle than water carrying sewage or creek runoff, which brings protective gear, disposal and more material removal.

  • Area affected

    Square footage and the number of rooms set labor and equipment counts. Drywall wicks water upward, so a wet floor often means a wet wall too.

  • Materials involved

    Hardwood, tile, carpet and drywall react differently. Some dry in place and some must be removed.

  • Drying days and equipment

    Every air mover and dehumidifier running adds to the total, and dense materials such as hardwood and subfloor run longer.

  • Rebuild scope

    Drying is one line item. Replacing drywall, insulation, flooring and paint afterward is a separate scope and is often the larger share.

Insurance and your water damage claim

Sudden and accidental damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph everything before cleanup. We cannot promise approval or payout.

After the drying

Water damage repair Lithia Springs homeowners need once everything is dry

Drying and repair are separate jobs and should be scoped separately. Water damage repair in Lithia Springs usually means cutting out wet drywall and insulation, replacing baseboards, patching ceilings, and refinishing or replacing flooring. Tell the crew early if you plan to file a claim, because removed materials should be photographed before they leave the house.

Permits depend on scope. Douglas County Building Permits and Inspections handles building, plumbing and electrical permits and asks contractors to show a state contractor license, a business license and a driver's license. Ask that office whether your repair needs a permit, especially if a pipe run or electrical circuit is replaced.

Mold enters the picture only when moisture lingers. Remediation and testing follow S520, and the Georgia Department of Public Health describes indoor air quality as not a regulated program, so ask any mold company how it works. Common Questions

How soon can a crew get to my Lithia Springs home?

Dispatch runs around the clock and same-day response is offered when available. Arrival depends on where crews are and on road and weather conditions, so no one can promise a clock time honestly. While you wait, close the main water valve, lift valuables and keep people and pets out of contaminated water.

Yes. The line at (943) 469-0138 is answered 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. A pipe that fails at 2 a.m. does the same damage as one that fails at noon, so dispatch does not wait for business hours.

Can I get a general estimate over the phone?

Dispatch can talk through what happened and explain what drives the price, including water category, area, materials, drying days and rebuild scope. A firm number needs moisture readings and a look at the site. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

What insurance documentation should I gather?

Take photos and video of the damage before cleanup, note the date, time and source, and list damaged items. Keep receipts for any temporary repairs and hold damaged belongings for the adjuster if you can. The Georgia Office of Commissioner of Insurance advises filing promptly, so read your policy for its notice window.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage?

Often it covers sudden and accidental events, such as a burst pipe or an overflowing appliance. Gradual leaks, neglect and flood are commonly excluded, and flood needs its own policy. Coverage varies by policy, so ask your insurer before assuming.

How long does it take to dry a home after water damage?

Many household losses need about three to five days of drying, but dense materials such as hardwood and subfloor, or a large wet area, can take longer. Technicians read moisture meters daily and stop only when the materials match dry reference readings.

Can water damage turn into a mold problem?

Mold needs moisture, so materials that stay wet for days raise the risk. Fast extraction and drying lower it, and remediation and testing follow the S520 standard. Questions about health effects belong with a physician.

Do you handle business and multi-unit water damage?

Yes. Shops, offices and apartment buildings are covered under commercial water damage restoration, where keeping downtime short and protecting inventory shape the plan. Call the same line.
